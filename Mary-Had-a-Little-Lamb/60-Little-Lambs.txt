
70 LITTLE LAMBS

Seventy exercises designed to teach computer music, using two songs.
The most common is "Mary Has a Little Lamb", since it's simple and recognizable enough.
The second is "Daisy Bell" since it was a classic song used in early musical voice synthesis.
Before proceeding with the exercises, what is our goal?
The primary goal is NOT audio synthesis, although audio synthesis is a relevant goal.
The primary goal of these exercises lies in using the computer to create and/or process "musical sequences".
Thus, our emphasis will overwhelmingly be on the control of MIDI data and patterns.
We will only rely on rudimentary forms of synthesis and use samples in some cases.
To understand our goal better, we should think ourselves as a conductor trying to tell the computer *how* to perform a score.
Where should it speed up? Where should it slow down? What chords should it use?
By the end of all this, the hope is that we will be able to generate a convincing enough performance of the songs of choice with just the click of a button.



=============================================================================
Lamb 01
    Create four synthdefs corresponding to the four notes of "Mary Had a Little Lamb".
Specify the notes in terms of raw frequencies -- you should in fact calculate these frequencies directly in code, recalling that A4 = 440Hz.
Use 12-TET. The scale factor is 2^(k/12) in case you forgot.
Play the melody manually using Ctrl+Enter then free. Remember to store the synth defs in variables so that you can free them!
    x = {}.play;
    x.free;
Now, read the documentation for "Env" objects, "Line" and "XLine".
These are envelope objects that allow us damp the amplitude of the notes over time.
Read the documentation for "doneAction" as well.
Once you have understood all this, move to exercise 2.

Lamb 02
    Create four synthdefs whose amplitudes are controlled by envelope UGens, and have done actions to automatically manage memory.
Specify the notes in terms of MIDI numbers instead of raw frequencies.
Another thing to do - the higher the pitch, the sharper the decay. We want the higher-pitched notes to sound slightly more "stiff" and hammer-like, while the lower-pitched notes ring out a bit more.
Note - for this exercise, we still play using Ctrl+Enter but this time we do not have to free the synthdef manually.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Lamb 03
    Research the documentation for any "sleep" functionality in SuperCollider.
If there exists one, then we will simulate timing by resting in between notes.
You will read in the documentation, how to create SynthDefs.
Create a SynthDef called "lamb" which plays the tune of "Mary Had a Little Lamb" when passed the "play" message i.e. we invoke lamb.play;
Its sole parameter should be "tempo", and this tells it how long to rest between notes.
Calculate the rests yourself (Hint: multiples of smallest unit of time in the song)


------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lamb 04
    Adjust the SynthDef so that the rhythm deviates slightly from the computed timing, in a random manner.
Also let the frequency of the notes deviate. Call this new SynthDef "drunklamb" if you want.
In this exercise, you will experiment with different types of randomness in SC.
Do some research on how you can implement an arbitrary probability distribution in SC.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Lamb 05: MIDI control
    Research on how MIDI works in general, then how MIDI works in SC.
If you have a MIDI controller e.g. on your phone, hook it up. If not see how you can use the computer keyboard for MIDI control.
    5a. Play the tune with your controller.
    5b. Create a synthdef such that whatever note you play triggers a transposed MHLL tune with your input note as the root. Let the tempo adjust with frequency - higher pitch means faster tempo.
